# Evaluation Principles

## Benchmarks are diagnostic instruments

A leaderboard result can support a narrow claim. It does not by itself establish world understanding.

## Separate capability layers

Future evaluation should distinguish:

1. **Cognitive diagnostics** — physics, identity, spatial, temporal, causal.
2. **World-model capability** — persistence, prediction, intervention, belief revision.
3. **Embodied utility** — whether the representation improves real planning, recovery and action.

## Prevent shortcut success

When practical, include:

- minimal pairs;
- background-only and static-frame probes;
- temporal shuffling;
- duplicate/scene leakage audits;
- examiner truth isolation.

## Report correctness and coverage

A system must not obtain apparent reliability by refusing nearly everything. Future protocols should report answer/task coverage together with correctness and unjustified assertions.

## Strong claims require public protocols

Terms such as `SOTA`, `solved`, `zero hallucination` or `fully understands` require a reproducible protocol appropriate to the claim.
